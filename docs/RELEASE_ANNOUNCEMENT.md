The clipboard forgot. We shipped a solution.

Today, Pasted 1.0 leaves the lab and enters your menu bar.

Your computer can simulate weather, render dragons, and put a person on a video call from an airport lounge. Its standard clipboard remembers one thing. We considered this an astonishing design choice.

Pasted remembers the rest.

## Same old copy. Brand new paste.

Copy text, links, images, screenshots, PDFs, files, and the strange fragments between them. Pasted catches everything quietly, gives it structure, and stays out of the way until the exact moment you need that thing from earlier.

Search it. Pin it. Put it in a Bin. Queue it. Transform it. Recover it from Trash after making a confident mistake.

There are Smart Bins, notes, revisions, Activity, local OCR, transcription, visual labels, Classifiers, and Transforms. There is also a CLI, because humans are not the only copycats anymore.

```sh
pasted search "that thing" --json
```

Found. Obviously.

## There’s no place like `~/`.

Clipboard history can contain passwords, code, medical forms, terrible first drafts, and the address of the surprise party.

So the core library lives on your machine. No cloud account. No telemetry. No subscription. Optional intelligence connections run only when explicitly enabled and used. Your work is not our dataset, and Pasted will not rent your own clipboard back to you.

Full Backup and Restore, portable transfer, App Lock, private-browser exclusions, signed updates, eight complete locales, and enough release receipts to make a clipboard app look oddly responsible are included.

## Get Pasted.

The primary build is a signed, notarized, and stapled universal app for macOS 13 or newer, on Apple Silicon and Intel.

**[Download Pasted 1.0](https://github.com/getpasted/pasted/releases/tag/v1.0.0)**

Or ask Homebrew to remember the details:

```sh
brew install --cask getpasted/tap/pasted
```

Linux x86_64 has an AppImage preview. Windows x86_64 has experimental unsigned builds. Every release includes checksums, updater signatures, third-party notices, and source plus exact-artifact SBOMs. Receipts, as promised.
